# Burn by Dawn — playtest sheet (M49)

For the operator. Put the game in front of **3 to 5 people who have never seen it**, one
at a time, and bring back where they got stuck. The first player tells us the most: if
you only manage one, that is still worth doing.

What M49 measures (ROADMAP.md, Phase 5): **how long to the first move, how long to the
first charge set, and what they ask.** The game times the first two for you; the third is
yours to write down.

Print one copy of "Per player" for each person.

---

## Before anyone sits down

1. Open the game with the record on:
   **https://davispiero.github.io/burn-by-dawn/?playtest**
   A slim dashed tab, PLAYTEST · recording, sits at the top of the page above the board.
   Check that the build in the left margin reads **M49** or later.
2. A desktop or laptop with a mouse, the window at least 1280 × 800. No phones or tablets.
3. Sound on, at a comfortable level.
4. Leave the page on the black title screen (BURN BY DAWN, click for your orders).
   **Reload the page for each new player**: that starts a fresh record. A reload in the
   middle of a player's game loses their record, so copy it first.

## What to say

Only this: *"It's a turn-based game about six paratroopers blowing up a bridge at night.
I can't help once you start. Please think out loud."*

Nothing about the course, the keys or what to click first. The game has to teach itself.

**Don't help.** If they are truly stuck for more than a minute, write down what they were
stuck on, then say just enough to unstick them, and mark it **HELPED**.

**Write down every question they ask, word for word**, even ones they answer themselves.
These are the most useful thing you will bring back.

## What the game records (you don't need to)

COPY RECORD (in the tab at the top) puts all of this on the clipboard as plain text:

- the time from the title screen to the first man selected, the first move, the first
  charge set and the first lesson passed
- each mission and lesson they start, how long it took, and how it ended
- **every wrong click in a lesson** (the lesson card shakes its head) and every umpire's
  whistle, counted by lesson and step: this shows where they trip
- each time they open **?**, turn the play tips on or off, undo, or start again

If the clipboard is not allowed, the text appears in a box under the button: click in it,
select all, copy. Paste it into a note for that player (Notes, an email to yourself,
anything) before the next player sits down.

## What to watch

Write down the moment, not your view of it ("lesson 5, kept clicking the sentry instead
of the hedge").

1. **The contents page.** Do they go to The Practice Ground or straight to
   France? Do they say why?
2. **The first minute.** The aim is a first move within a minute. What do they do first?
   What do they try to click?
3. **In the course**, if they take it:
   - Do they read the lesson card, or click around the board first?
   - Do they find NEXT, STEP BACK, START AGAIN and COURSE MENU when they need them?
   - When the umpire blows the whistle, do they understand why?
   - Do they go through the lessons in order, skip some, or stop? Where do they stop,
     and do they say why?
4. **In a mission** (France, or whichever they pick):
   - Do they find the hover readout and the red risk dots without being told?
   - Do they pack the parachutes on turn 1?
   - Do they find where a charge goes, and set one? How long did it take them?
   - Do they notice the alert dial rise? Do they call the RAF diversion?
   - Do they know where the way out is, and when to go?
5. **The play tips** (on by default). Do they read the red pen's notes? Do they turn them
   off?
6. **The ? pages.** Do they open them? When? Do the moving pictures help?
7. **Where they get confused, bored or annoyed.** Note the moment and what was on screen.
8. **Where they grin, swear or lean in.** The same.

Stop after about 30 minutes, or when they want to stop.

## After (5 minutes, ask in this order)

1. What happened, in your own words?
2. What was the best moment? The most frustrating?
3. Was there anything you wanted to do and couldn't find?
4. If they took the course: did it help, and was any lesson too long or too obvious?
   If they skipped it: why?
5. Would you play again?

Don't defend the game or explain it until they've answered everything.

## Bringing it back

Start a new session with `/notes` and paste in, for each player: the record, the
questions they asked, the HELPED moments and your notes. The record says *where*; your
notes say *why*. That session sorts them and proposes the fixes, as M49b.
